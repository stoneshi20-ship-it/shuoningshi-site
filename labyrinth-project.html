Designing an experience, not just an object

Labyrinth

A desk ritual that gives a busy executive three minutes to breathe, trace, and come back down.
01
The intent

We set out to shape a moment, not a machine.

A meditation app asks for your attention. Labyrinth asks for almost nothing. You reach out, your finger falls into the groove, a warm light breathes ahead of it, and for three minutes the day goes quiet. That feeling is the product; the ceramic disc is just how it reaches you.

MID-DAY RESET
02
The problem

Executives don't lack breaks. They lack a break they're allowed to take.

The real barrier isn't stress-relief technique — those exist. It's an on-ramp low-friction and socially invisible enough that a senior person will actually use it, at their desk, in the three minutes they have, without looking like they've checked out.

  • Fragmented — 3–8 min between meetings, easily interrupted.
  • Low-key — open, glass-walled offices; the reset has to stay discreet.
  • Zero setup — no pairing, no screen, one gesture.

User study

What people told us — and what it changed

  • "I never actually step away." → a reset that happens right at the desk, in under 3 minutes.
  • "Meditation apps? Too long, another screen." → no screen, no audio — a physical object.
  • "It has to be low-key." → discreet by design; the action reads as touching a desk object, not "doing wellness."
  • "It sits on my desk — it has to look the part." → aesthetics & material matter: a considered, tactile object with taste.
  • Hands already fidget with pens under stress. → give them a designed groove to trace instead.
  • "I can't just switch off on command." → the light leads the breath for you — nothing to figure out.

DISCOVER → DEFINE
04
What we learned

Six truths that became design constraints

01

It has to be low-key

In a shared/glass office it must be discreet — the action reads as touching a desk object, not "doing wellness."

02

Apps get abandoned

"Too long, needs headphones, another screen." Bar: no screen, no audio, <3 min.

03

Hands already fidget

Pens, phones. There's a behaviour to redirect, not a habit to invent.

04

Guide me, don't ask me to try

By mid-day there's no willpower left to "relax on command" — people want something to follow, so the light leads the breath for them.

05

Stop & resume anytime

Breaks get cut short constantly — it has to pick up and put down instantly, with no timer, no "session failed" guilt.

06

Taste & material matter

It sits on their desk — aesthetics, finish and material carry the value; it has to look and feel considered.

INSIGHTS
05
The interaction

One gesture. Trace, and slow down.

Your finger falls into a single spiral groove and follows a warm light inward as you breathe in, outward as you breathe out. The groove limits your speed, so you can't rush. Keep pace with the light and the path glows warmer — a quiet "you've got it."

08
Light & build

The calm is engineered.

  • LEDs under the plate feed a diffuser in the groove floor — the light lives inside the channel, no visible bulbs.
  • A soft point travels the groove to set the breath; warm amber glow, never a cold notification.
  • Warming layer + weighted base; USB-C, lift-to-wake.
