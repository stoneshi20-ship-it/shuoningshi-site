# Labyrinth — Walkthrough Script (spoken)

~4–5 min. Casual, first person. [ ] = stage cue. Say it like you're talking to one person, not reading.

---

### 1 · Cover
"Hey — so this is **Labyrinth**. The brief was simple on paper: a desk device that helps busy executives find a little calm in the middle of the day. But the more I dug in, the more I realized the hard part isn't relaxation — it's everything around it. Let me walk you through it."
[click]

### 2 · Intent
"First, the mindset I went in with. I didn't want to design *a machine*. I wanted to design **a moment** — three quiet minutes someone can actually take at their desk. So through the whole project, the question wasn't 'what features does it have,' it was 'what does that pause *feel* like.'"
[click]

### 3 · Problem
"Here's the real problem. Executives don't actually lack breaks — they lack a break they feel *allowed* to take. Their day is chopped into three-to-eight-minute gaps between meetings. They're often in a glass office, so anything they do is on show. And they've got zero patience for setup. So the bar was really high: it has to be fast, low-key, and basically invisible."
[click]

### 4 · User study + demo
"To get there I kept the research small but real — some desk research, a short survey, a couple of interviews, and usability testing on the prototype. And every quote pointed at a design move. People said 'I never actually step away' — okay, so it has to happen *right at the desk*. 'Meditation apps? too long, another screen' — so, no screen, no audio. 'It has to be low-key' — so it looks like you're just touching an object, not doing wellness.
And here's the prototype — [**demo**: trace the spiral with your finger] — you just follow this warm light. It moves inward as you breathe in, back out as you breathe out. That's the whole thing. No instructions, nothing to learn."
[demo ~10 sec, then click]

### 5 · Insights
"Those conversations boiled down to six truths — and I treated each one as a hard constraint, not a nice-to-have. It has to be low-key. Apps get abandoned, so no screen. Hands are already fidgeting under stress, so give them something better to do. People can't just *decide* to relax — so something external should lead. It has to survive constant interruption — stop and resume, no guilt. And last, taste and material actually matter, because this thing lives on their desk."
[click]

### 6 · The interaction
"So the interaction is one gesture. Your finger falls into a single spiral groove and follows the light. The groove literally limits how fast you can move — so you slow down whether you mean to or not. And when you keep pace with the light, the path glows a little warmer. That's the only feedback — a quiet 'you've got it.'"
[click]

### 7 · Form
"Form-wise, I wanted a soft pebble, not a device. It's a smooth white-ceramic saucer, about the size of your palm, rounded everywhere — no sharp edges anywhere. One spiral groove that flows into a little dish in the center. And no buttons on show: you just lift it to wake it."
[click]

### 8 · Context
"This is what it looks like on a desk. It doesn't read as tech — it reads as a quiet object you'd want to keep there. That was the whole point: it invites you in without demanding anything."
[click]

### 9 · Light & build
"Under the calm, it is engineered. There's a ring of LEDs feeding a diffuser in the floor of the groove, so the light looks like it lives *inside* the channel — you never see a bulb. A soft point travels the groove to pace your breath, in warm amber, never a cold notification light. Plus a gentle warming layer and a weighted base."
[click]

### 10 · Materials
"One ritual, but it can suit any desk — ceramic, marble, an amber resin, aluminium, walnut, dark stone. Same object, different personalities."
[click]

### 11 · Closing
"So to wrap up — I didn't really design a disc. I designed **three quiet minutes**. Next steps would be a physical prototype with the real engraved groove and light, and validating the calming effect with heart-rate data. But the core idea is here: something small and beautiful that gives a busy person permission to breathe. Thanks — happy to take questions."

---

**Tips**
- Slow down on slide 2 and slide 11 — those are the emotional bookends.
- Actually do the demo on slide 4; don't just talk about it. Let it breathe for a few seconds in silence.
- If you're short on time, you can merge 7+8 (form + context) into one beat.
