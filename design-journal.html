2026 · Aug 14 — Brief & framing

The real constraints (why this brief is hard)

"Mid-day relaxation for busy executives" sounds soft, but the context is unforgiving:

  • Fragmented time — the window is 3–8 min between meetings, and it can be interrupted.
  • Composure — often a glass / open office; it can't look weird to use.
  • Zero setup — no pairing, no fiddling. One touch to start and stop.
  • Status object — it lives on an executive's desk, so it must read as an object / artifact, not a gadget (material, weight, finish).

The mechanisms that actually calm a nervous system are few: extended exhale (vagal tone), warming the palms (parasympathetic), HRV biofeedback, single-sensory focus, and weight / grounding. Good design = do one or two of these extremely well, hidden inside a dignified desk object.

2026 · Aug 14 — Concept directions

Six directions + a flagship

1 · Breathing Stone

A sculptural object that slowly "breathes" (swells / glows). You match it for 3 min; exhale runs longer. Can adapt pace from Apple Watch HRV.

2 · Warm Palm

A warm ceramic/stone "pebble" you cradle for 90s — hand-warming triggers the parasympathetic system; looks like you're just holding something. Flip for a cool, alerting side.

3 · Timed Scent Reset

A desk diffuser tied to the calendar — after a hard meeting it offers a 5-min scent + soft light burst, then stops itself.

4 · Grounding fidget

A premium damped press/spin object — a satisfying tactile outlet for anxious hands; grounding, screen-free.

5 · Sound Dome × AirPods

The object is the "do not disturb" signal (twist = 5-min mode, light dims); private soundscape plays through AirPods.

6 · Eye rest / micro-nap

Dims the local desk light + a 20-min closed-eye timer with a gentle haptic wake — an off-switch for the eyes and brain.

Flagship: merge 1 + 2 + HRV into one desk reset object — cover it with your palm → it lights, breathes, and warms; HRV adapts the pace; the calendar suggests a 3-min break between intense meetings; it ends silently.

2026 · Aug 14 — Idea: the finger labyrinth ("迷宫尺")

Trace-to-calm: a finger labyrinth on the desk

Idea on the table: build a finger-maze / labyrinth into a desk object you trace with a fingertip. I like this a lot — possibly the most desk-appropriate, least awkward, least tech-dependent mechanism of the set.

  • The mechanism holds up. Slowly tracing a grooved path — repetitive, rhythmic, goal-less — lowers arousal. It's a real mindfulness tool. It also just looks like a hand resting on a beautiful object.
  • Critical call: labyrinth, not maze. A single-path (unicursal) labyrinth / meander soothes; a maze with dead-ends frustrates (problem-solving, failure). Go with a spiral / meander groove.
  • It merges with the flagship. Trace inward = inhale, outward = exhale; or a light travels the groove to set the breathing pace. Add palm warmth + a subtle damped drag in the channel.
  • Form: an engraved metal / stone plate that doubles as a coaster / paperweight / desk sculpture — zero setup, always at hand, has presence.
  • Watch-outs: (1) novelty fades → win it back with material + finish (groove depth, micro-resistance, warmth); (2) it must feel expensive, not like a toy.
Decision leaning: make the traceable labyrinth the primary interaction, and fuse it with breath pacing — one engraved plate you trace at breathing rhythm, optionally lit.

2026 · Aug 14 — Interactive prototype

Built a browser demo of the trace-to-calm interaction

To feel the interaction before any hardware, I built it as a Playground tool (labyrinth.html): a single-path spiral groove you trace with finger / cursor, with a breathing light that travels the path to pace you.

  • Core loop confirmed: the groove "limits your speed" so you naturally slow down; following the light sets the breath (in on the way to center, out on the way back).
  • In-sync feedback: when your dot matches the light, the traced segment glows warm — a quiet "you've got it" signal.
  • Pace tuned slower: first pass (in 4s / out 6s) felt too fast on screen because the path is long. Settled on in 5s · out 7s with longer holds (~14s cycle).
Open question for the physical object: does a real engraved groove + moving light band hold attention longer than the screen version? Next: try 1 / 3 / 5-min nested path lengths, and test the double-spiral (separate in/out paths).
